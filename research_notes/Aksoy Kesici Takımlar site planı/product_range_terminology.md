## 1. Product category tree (Turkish market names + English) and filters

### Takeaway
Turkish dealers and the Turkish sites of the big brands organize the range by operation: Tornalama, Kesme & Kanal Açma, Diş Açma/Diş Çekme, Frezeleme, Delik Delme, Delik İşleme (bara/rayba) and Takım Tutucular. The everyday trade words are "kater" (toolholder), "uç"/"kesici uç"/"elmas uç" (insert), "parmak freze" (end mill), "tarama çakısı" (face mill), "kılavuz/klavuz" (tap), "pens" and "pens tutucu" (ER collet and chuck), "U-drill/takma uçlu matkap", "rayba" and "bara/baralama".

## 2. ISO 1832 indexable insert designation – decoder tables

### Takeaway
An ISO 1832 code (e.g., CNMG 120408-MA) is read position by position: shape, clearance, tolerance, hole/chipbreaker type, size, thickness, corner radius, then optional edge condition and hand, then a manufacturer suffix after the hyphen. Positions 1–7 are mandatory. The chipbreaker suffix (MA, MP, PM, -M…) is NOT standardized and must come from each brand's table.

### Cited Findings
- **Structure.** The full designation has 13 symbols, of which only the first 7 are mandatory — [search extract citing Sumitomo catalog B / machiningdoctor](https://www.sumitool.com/en/downloads/cutting-tools/general-catalog/assets/pdf/b.pdf); [Machining Doctor – ISO inserts](https://www.machiningdoctor.com/isoturn/). The current standard edition is ISO 1832:2017 ("Indexable inserts for cutting tools — Designation") — [ISO 1832:2017 sample](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/69202/2c2fc914eec64de0a093ca7175749223/ISO-1832-2017.pdf)
- **Pos. 1 – Shape (included corner angle εr)**:
  - C = rhombic 80°, D = rhombic 55°, S = square 90°, T = triangle 60°, V = rhombic 35°, W = trigon 80°, R = round, K = parallelogram 55° — [Cutwel – ISO Code System](https://www.cutwel.co.uk/blog/learn-the-turning-tool-iso-code-system); [Industrial Monitor Direct – shape codes](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/cnc-turning-insert-geometry-shape-codes-angles-selection-guide)
  - A = parallelogram 85°, B = parallelogram 82°, E = rhombic 75°, H = hexagon 120°, L = rectangle 90°, M = rhombic 86°, O = octagon 135°, P = pentagon 108° — [CTE – Identification System for Indexable Inserts](https://ctemag.com/articles/understanding-identification-system-indexable-inserts/); [FM Carbide – Insert Designation Chart](https://fmcarbide.com/pages/insert-designation-chart-1)
- **Pos. 2 – Normal clearance angle (αn)**: A = 3°, B = 5°, C = 7°, D = 15°, E = 20°, F = 25°, G = 30°, N = 0°, P = 11°, O = other (special specification). N = negative insert, held tilted in the holder. Other letters = positive inserts with ground-in side clearance — [AIMS Industrial – Indexable Insert Guide](https://aimsindustrial.com.au/blogs/product-guides/indexable-insert-guide)
- **Pos. 3 – Tolerance class** (m = corner/edge position, d = inscribed circle, s = thickness):

  | Class | m | d | s |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | A | ±0.005 | ±0.025 | ±0.025 |
  | F | ±0.005 | ±0.013 | ±0.025 |
  | C | ±0.013 | ±0.025 | ±0.025 |
  | H | ±0.013 | ±0.013 | ±0.025 |
  | E | ±0.025 | ±0.025 | ±0.025 |
  | G | ±0.025 | ±0.025 | ±0.13 |
  | M | size dependent | ±0.05 to ±0.15 | ±0.13 |

  Source — [AIMS Industrial](https://aimsindustrial.com.au/blogs/product-guides/indexable-insert-guide); H class: [refmanual TNMA 16T308 decode](https://refmanual.com/inserts/tnma-16t308/)
- **M class, size-dependent values** (Mitsubishi tolerance page):
  - d: ±0.05 for IC 6.35 and 9.525; ±0.08 for IC 12.70 (the extract also listed 19.05 under ±0.08 — see conflict note)
  - m: ±0.08 at 6.35/9.525; ±0.13 at 12.70; ±0.15 at 19.05; ±0.18 at 25.40
  - s: ±0.13
  - M is the class for pressed (non-ground) general-production inserts
  - Source — [Mitsubishi – Symbol for Tolerance Class](https://mitsubishicarbide.net/contents/mhg/ru/html/product/product_guide/information/turning/seido.html); [Mitsubishi – insert identification](https://www.mmc-carbide.com/permanent/courses/85/insert-turning-identification-code.html)
- **Pos. 4 – Fixing hole / chipbreaker type**:

  | Code | Meaning |
  |---|---|
  | A | cylindrical hole, no chipbreaker |
  | G | cylindrical hole, chipbreaker both faces (double-sided) |
  | M | cylindrical hole, chipbreaker one face |
  | N | no hole, no chipbreaker |
  | R | no hole, chipbreaker one face |
  | T | hole with 40–60° countersink, chipbreaker one face |
  | W | hole with 40–60° countersink, no chipbreaker |

  Source — [CADEM – CNC Insert Identification](https://cadem.com/cnc-insert-identification-code/); [KENFA – How to read ISO insert designation](https://www.kenfatools.com/buyer-guides/read-iso-turning-insert-designation/). The extract described W as "double countersink" and in the same breath confused it with the W trigon shape, so treat that wording as unreliable (see Gaps).
- **Pos. 5 – Size.** The two digits give cutting-edge length in mm. ## 5. Carbide grades, coatings (CVD vs PVD), cermet, CBN, PCD – glossary basis

### Takeaway
CVD coatings (thick, high-temperature, typically TiCN + Al2O3 + TiN) dominate steel and cast-iron turning thanks to wear and crater resistance. PVD coatings (thin, low-temperature, typically TiAlN/AlTiN, TiN) keep edges sharp and tough, so they suit milling, drilling, grooving, stainless, superalloys and sticky materials. Cermets (TiC/TiCN-based) suit finishing steel. CBN suits hardened steel and cast iron. PCD suits non-ferrous materials.

### Cited Findings
- CVD (Kimyasal Buhar Çökeltme) is deposited at about 700–1050 °C. It bonds very well to carbide and gives high wear resistance. CVD stacks combine Ti(C,N), Al2O3 and TiN. Al2O3 is chemically inert, so it resists crater wear — [Çeyrek Mühendis – CVD ve PVD Kaplama Nedir?](https://ceyrekmuhendis.com/cvd-ve-pvd-kaplama-nedir/); [Çelik Kesici Takımlar – PVD mi CVD mi?](https://www.celikkesicitakimlar.com/pvd-vs-cvd-kaplama-farklari-nedir/); [Eminoğlu Teknik – Kesici Takım Kaplama Teknolojileri](https://www.eminogluteknik.com/haberdetay/kesici-takim-kaplama-teknolojileri)
- PVD (Fiziksel Buhar Çökeltme) is deposited at about 400–600 °C. TiAlN adds high hardness and oxidation resistance. PVD-coated tools are tough while keeping sharp edges, and are recommended for adhesive (sticky) materials such as aluminium — same sources as above
- Kyocera produces cermet, coated carbide, coated super-micrograin carbide, carbide, ceramics, PCD and CBN. Typical cermet constituents are TiC, TiN, TiCN and NbC. ## 6. Formulas for the on-site calculators (turning, milling, drilling, tapping)

### Takeaway
The core formulas are standard and agree across Sandvik, Mitsubishi and Turkish sources:
- n = 1000·vc / (π·D)
- vc = π·D·n / 1000
- turning: vf = fn·n; Q = vc·ap·fn
- milling: vf = fz·z·n; Q = ap·ae·vf / 1000
- drilling: vf = fn·n
- tapping: vf = P·n
- power: Pc = vc·ap·fn·kc / 60 000

### Cited Findings
- **Sandvik turning:**
  - Cutting speed vc = (Dm·π·n)/1000 [m/min]
  - Spindle speed n = (vc·1000)/(π·Dm) [rpm]
  - Metal removal rate Q = vc·ap·fn [cm³/min]
  - Net power Pc = (vc·ap·fn·kc)/(60·10³) [kW]
  - Symbols: Dm = machined diameter (mm); fn = feed per rev (mm/r); ap = depth of cut (mm); kc = specific cutting force (N/mm²)
  - Source — [Sandvik – General Turning Formulas and Definitions](https://www.sandvik.coromant.com/en-us/knowledge/machining-formulas-definitions/general-turning-formulas-definitions); [Sandvik turning formulas PDF](https://cdn.sandvik.coromant.com/files/sitecollectiondocuments/services/metal-cutting-e-learning/formulas-and-definitions/formulas-and-deinitions-for-turning-metric-enu.pdf) (search extract)
- Sandvik also publishes separate formula pages for milling, drilling, boring and threading — [Milling formulas](https://www.sandvik.coromant.com/en-us/knowledge/machining-formulas-definitions/milling-formulas-definitions); [Drilling formulas](https://www.sandvik.coromant.com/en-us/knowledge/machining-formulas-definitions/drilling-formulas-definitions); [Threading formulas](https://www.sandvik.coromant.com/en-us/knowledge/machining-formulas-definitions/threading-formulas-definitions); [Boring formulas](https://www.sandvik.coromant.com/en-us/knowledge/machining-formulas-definitions/boring-formulas-definitions) (pages exist; content not fetched)
- **Turkish formula pages:**
  - Kesme hızı V = 3.14·D·N/1000 (m/dak.), with D = takım/iş çapı (mm) and N = devir sayısı (dev/dak.)
  - Devir n = 1000·V/(π·d)
  - Tabla ilerleme hızı Vf = n·fz·Z (mm/dk), with fz = diş başına ilerleme (mm/diş) and Z = ağız sayısı
  - Turning feed is in mm/dev
  - Source — [Makine Eğitimi – Tornada Kesme Hızı ve Devir Sayısı](https://www.makinaegitimi.com/tornada-kesme-hizi-ve-devir-sayisi/); [AKKO – Frezeleme İşleminde Devir ve İlerleme Hesabı (PDF)](https://www.akko.com.tr/UserFiles/FileGallery/FREZELEME%20%C4%B0%C5%9ELEM%C4%B0NDE%20DEV%C4%B0R%20ve%20%C4%B0LERLEME%20HESABI.pdf); [SMT – freze devir/ilerleme](http://www.smt.com.tr/teknik.php); [Taha Özel – Kesme Parametreleri Hesaplama Rehberi 2026](https://tahaozel.com/kesme-parametreleri-hesaplama-rehberi/); [Çelik Kesici Takımlar – İlerleme ve Devir Hesaplama Rehberi](https://www.celikkesicitakimlar.com/cnc-operatorleri-icin-ilerleme-ve-devir-hesaplama-rehberi-formuller-ve-ipuclari/)

Implementation notes:
- D for turning is the workpiece diameter being cut. For milling and drilling it is the tool diameter. For ball-nose milling at shallow ap, use the effective diameter De = 2·√(ap·(DC − ap)) **[UNVERIFIED, Sandvik milling page not read]**.
- Clamp n to a user-entered machine max rpm and show a warning (e.g., "Tezgâhınızın maksimum devri: … dev/dak").
- CSS (G96) on lathes: show that n rises as D falls and recommend a G50 limit (Turkish operators use "G50 devir sınırı" on Fanuc-type controls; this is editorial and not cited).
- For rigid tapping, feed per rev must equal the pitch exactly (G84 F = P × S in mm/min mode, or F = P in mm/rev mode). This is editorial, follows from vf = P·n.
- kc defaults by ISO group are needed for the power calculation (e.g., P ≈ 1500–2000 N/mm², M ≈ 2000–2300, K ≈ 1000–1300, N ≈ 500–800, S ≈ 2500–3200, H ≈ 3000–4500) **[UNVERIFIED, indicative only; use the manufacturer's kc1.1 tables]**.

## 8. Metric tap drill sizes ("kılavuz matkap çapı") – coarse and fine

### Takeaway
For cut taps the shop rule is: drill Ø = nominal Ø − pitch (e.g., M10×1.5 → 8.5 mm). This reproduces the standard coarse-thread table (M3 → 2.5 … M24 → 21.0) and fine-thread values (M24×2 → 22.0, M24×1.5 → 22.5, M24×1 → 23.0). Form (roll) taps need a larger hole and a separate table.

### Cited Findings
- Practical formula: Matkap Çapı = Diş Üstü Çapı − Adım (Hatve), e.g., M10×1.5 → 10 − 1.5 = 8.5 mm. Catalog values take precedence for fine threads, short series or special alloys — [Taha Özel – Metrik Diş Tablosu](https://tahaozel.com/metrik-dis-tablosu/); [Ersin Alkan – Metrik Diş Tablosu](https://www.ersinalkan.com/teknik-bilgiler/metrik-dis-tablosu/) (search extract)
- M3 → 2.5 mm; M24 → 21.0 mm. Fine: M24×2 → 22.0, M24×1.5 → 22.5, M24×1 → 23.0; M3×0.35 → 2.70 mm — [Lazer CRM – Metrik Klavuz Öncesi Delik Çapları](https://lazercrm.com.tr/muhendislik-araclari/metrik-klavuz-on-delik-caplari); [eitasarim – Kılavuz için Matkap Çapları](https://www.eitasarim.com/2019/05/metrik-standart-vida-tablosu-matkap-caplari/); [Mühendis Portal – Kılavuz ve cıvata tablosu](https://muhendisportal.com/kilavuz-ve-civata-tablosu) (search extract). The same extract also listed "M24×2.5 → 21.5 mm". M24×2.5 is not a standard ISO pitch (coarse M24 is 3.0), so this looks like an extraction error.
- The most common tap-breakage causes: wrong drill diameter, misalignment (eksen kaçıklığı) and chip packing. The most common error is using a straight-flute tap in a blind hole — [Ata Teknik – Kılavuz Kırılmasının En Önemli 10 Nedeni](https://atateknik.com.tr/blog/kilavuz-kirilmasinin-en-onemli-10-nedeni-ve-cozumu/); [Maktoloji – Kılavuz Neden Kırılır?](https://www.maktoloji.com/2018/09/klavuz-neden-krlr.html)
